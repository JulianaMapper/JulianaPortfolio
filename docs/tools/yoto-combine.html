    Good to know

    Only what has to be joined is joined. 150 files become 100 tracks: the shortest neighbors pair up and the rest stay as they are. A joined track is called “Chapter 3 / Chapter 4”. 100 files or fewer go on as they are.
    Yoto’s limits: 100 tracks and 5 hours per card, and 60 minutes or 100 MB per track. Over 5 hours, this makes two or more cards (“Part 1 of 2”). A single file over 60 minutes needs splitting before it can go on.
    Quality only changes when it has to. MP3s are joined as they are. A track that would go over 100 MB, or that joins non-MP3 files, is re-made as a mono MP3. Yoto turns every track into mono anyway, so it sounds the same on the player.
    Everything stays between you and Yoto. Your files are read and joined in this browser and sent straight to Yoto. Nothing goes to me, and there are no analytics or trackers.
    Then link it to a card. The new playlist shows up under Make Your Own in the Yoto app. Open it and tap Link to Card to put it on a blank card.
    Only use audio you have the right to. Your own recordings, or audio you’ve bought for your family’s own use.
